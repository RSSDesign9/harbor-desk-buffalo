Five more primary typefaces for Harbor Desk, each paired with Work Sans

Same B-12 moment, same sizes and density rules as round one, five new ideas. None of them repeats a direction from round one. Atkinson Hyperlegible Next stays bookmarked as the benchmark to beat.

Not in shadcnAll five were checked against font-definitions.ts (26 families) and are on Google Fonts under the OFL.
Wide weight rangeFour cover 100–900. National Park covers 200–800. Three have a second axis that changes their character: flare, width or sharpness.

06 · Park signage
National Park

Drawn from the routed wooden signs of the US National Park Service

Jeremy Shellhorn, Andrea Herstowski, Ben Hoepner and others · weights 200–800 · added to Google Fonts April 2025 · OFL
Ag200
Ag400
Ag600
Dock B800

Why it fits. It's based on the brown signs routed into wood at every American park, boat launch and public waterfront. The Erie Basin and Buffalo River marinas sit inside public waterfront parks, so this is the lettering people already pass on the way to their slip. The routing gives it soft corners and a carved feel that no SaaS grotesque has.

Who it serves. Boaters and seasonal staff for whom the marina is leisure, not an office. It feels like a place rather than a piece of software, while staying a plain sans-serif that holds up at 14 px.

Distinctiveness. It's rare: Google Fonts popularity rank #764, and only on the service since April 2025. A hiring manager won't have seen it in another portfolio.

Watch. There's no italic, so the water-in-italic idea doesn't work here. Water labels need letter spacing instead. Some will also see it as "outdoorsy", so keep it to names, headings and counts.

07 · Brass nameplate
Commissioner

A clean sans with a flare axis that adds engraved-looking flared terminals as text gets bigger

Kostas Bartsokas · weights 100–900 · FLAR 0–100 · VOLM 0–100 · slnt 0 to −12 · OFL
Ag200 · flare 0
Ag500 · flare 100
Ag800 · flare 100
Lake Effect600 · flare 100

Why it fits. Boats carry engraved metal: builder's plates, hull ID plates, the brass name board on the stern. At flare 100, Commissioner gets those flared, chiselled endings in vessel names and headings. At flare 0 it's a quiet sans for labels. Personality becomes one dial tied to text size.

Who it serves. Owners and managers, people who care about a boat's name and history. It also gives dock staff a calm, unflared label style at small sizes, so density doesn't suffer.

System angle. --flare-display: 100, --flare-ui: 0. One family, two voices, no second download.

Watch. At flare 100 and low weight it drifts toward wedding stationery. Keep flare to weights of 500 and up, and don't let it reach buttons.

08 · Registry paperwork
Bitter

A slab serif built for screens, the voice of berth agreements and registration forms

Sol Matas, Huerta Tipográfica · weights 100–900 + italics · OFL
Ag200
Ag500
Ag800
Lake Effect600 italic

Why it fits. Harbor Desk is a registry, and registries are paper: berth agreements, state registrations, the clipboard in the dock office. Slab serifs are the typewriter-and-form voice of that world. Bitter was drawn for reading on screens, so its slabs stay crisp at 15 px on a dark panel, where a bookish serif would go thin.

Who it serves. The manager reconciling records. It suggests "this is the official record" without looking like legal software, and the italic keeps round one's chart rule for water and vessel names.

Versus Newsreader. It's sturdier and more mechanical. It has less charm but survives small sizes, dark backgrounds and lower-quality tablet screens better.

Watch. It's popular (rank #114) and was everywhere in 2012–2016 blogs. Only the italic-for-water rule and heavy weights stop it from feeling dated.

09 · Helm instruments
Saira

A squared technical sans with a width axis from 50% to 125%, inspired by marine electronics

Omnibus-Type, Héctor Gatti · weights 100–900 + italics · wdth 50–125 · OFL
Ag200
Ag500
Ag800
126 · 48500 · 62%

Why it fits. Every boat has a helm with a chartplotter, depth sounder and VHF, all of which use squared, condensed numerals made to be read at a glance on a moving deck. Saira brings that instrument feel to KPIs and slip numbers. The width axis does the density work: 62% for counts, 118% for small caps labels.

Who it serves. Dock staff and dockmasters who read numbers more than words. Squared numerals stay distinct at a distance, and the narrow width packs three KPI tiles where two used to fit.

Fit with 3D. Of all ten options, it sits most naturally over a real-time 3D scene. It looks like an overlay on a live instrument.

Watch. This is the riskiest with a picky reviewer: squared sans-serifs can read as gaming or motorsport. It's also common (rank #50). Keep it for numbers and headings only; Work Sans has to carry all prose.

10 · Cut vinyl
Geologica

A geometric grotesque with a sharpness axis that cuts square corners into curves, like plotter-cut vinyl

Monokrom (Sindre Bremnes, Frode Helland) · weights 100–900 · SHRP 0–100 · CRSV 0–1 · slnt 0 to −12 · OFL
Ag200 · sharp 0
Ag500 · sharp 100
Ag800 · sharp 100
NY 8110 BL700 · sharp 100

Why it fits. Boat names and registration numbers are cut from vinyl sheet and applied by hand. Federal rules (33 CFR 173.27) require bow numbers in "plain vertical block characters" at least 3 inches tall. Geologica at sharp 0 is that plain block letter, for IDs and labels. At sharp 100 the curves get cut corners, which gives headings and counts the look of fresh cut lettering.

Who it serves. Everyone fairly evenly. It has a large x-height, open shapes and is strong at 13 px. Of this round it comes closest to Atkinson's legibility while having more visible character.

Craft detail. The cursive axis turns on single-storey a and y for water labels, a softer way to say "this is water" when there's no true italic.

Watch. At sharp 100 and heavy weight it can look like a tech-startup display face. Hold the sharpness to headings and counts, at sizes of 24 px and up.

Recommendation: 06 National Park, tested against the bookmark

National Park has the most honest story of the ten: it's the lettering of American public waterfronts, rarely seen in software and new to Google Fonts. It gives Harbor Desk a sense of place with no extra ornament, which is what a hiring manager remembers.

A hybrid worth trying: National Park for names, headings and counts, Work Sans for UI and prose, and Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono in place of IBM Plex Mono for hull IDs and slip numbers. That's still three families, and your bookmark goes where legibility matters most. Runner-up: 10 Geologica, the closest to Atkinson's legibility with more character.
