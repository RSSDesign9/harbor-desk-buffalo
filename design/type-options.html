Five primary typefaces for Harbor Desk, each paired with Work Sans

Every specimen below is the same B-12 slip-review moment, set at real product sizes. The primary typeface carries what a dockmaster scans for: slip, vessel name, counts, the water. Work Sans carries everything they read. IBM Plex Mono stays for timestamps and record IDs.

Not in shadcnChecked against shadcn-ui/ui · font-definitions.ts today. Its 26 families are all excluded, including Space Grotesk, Instrument, Manrope, IBM Plex Sans and Montserrat.
Wide weight rangeEvery pick is a variable Google Font covering at least 200–800. Three also carry a second axis (optical size, width or casual) that does real work in a dense UI.
Less dense than todayMono uppercase labels are cut back to IDs, times and chips. Section labels move to the primary face, body goes up from 14 to 15 px, and rows get 4 px more air.
01 · Great Lakes signage
Big Shoulders

Condensed civic grotesque with an optical-size axis and a stencil cut

Patric King · weights 100–900 · opsz 10–72 · Stencil companion · Google Fonts, OFL
Ag200
Ag400
Ag600
Ag800
B-12Stencil 900

Why it fits. It was drawn for Chicago's civic identity, which places it among Great Lakes industrial cities like Buffalo, with their freighters and grain elevators. It reads as dock signage, not as a startup dashboard. Being condensed, it takes back width: B-12, "Lake Effect" and 3-digit counts all fit in less room, so panels can breathe without growing.

Who it serves. Dock staff glancing at a tablet from arm's length. The tall caps and open counters hold up at 40 px+, and the optical-size axis tightens spacing at 13 px so small caps labels don't smear.

Stencil is kept for physical dock letters in the 3D scene only, the way a real marina paints them on pilings.

Watch. Condensed caps turn shouty in quantity. Keep caps to labels of 13 px or more and set vessel names in mixed case. Never use it for paragraphs.

02 · Hull lettering
Archivo

Grotesque with a width axis from 62% to 125%, so density becomes a setting

Omnibus-Type · weights 100–900 + italics · wdth 62–125 · Google Fonts, OFL
Ag200 · 125%
Ag500 · 125%
Ag800 · 125%
048700 · 72%

Why it fits. Extended caps are how boats and docks get lettered: registration numbers on the bow, names on the transom, the wide HARBOR DESK wordmark already in the logo. Archivo gives you that at 125%, then pulls in to 72% for KPI numerals and table headers where space is tight. One family handles both ends.

Who it serves. The manager at a desktop scanning 126 slips. A narrow width in tables, a wide one for headings: the eye separates "where am I" from "what's in this row" by shape alone.

Design-system angle. Width becomes a token (--w-display: 125, --w-dense: 72), which is an easy story to tell a hiring manager.

Watch. This is the only option that is also a grotesque like Work Sans. At normal width the two blur together. It only works if Archivo stays at the width extremes, never 100%.

03 · Glare-proof
Atkinson Hyperlegible Next

Braille Institute's low-vision typeface, rebuilt in 2025 with seven weights and a mono

Braille Institute, Applied Design Works · 200–800 + italics · Mono companion · Google Fonts, OFL
Il1200
0O8B400
NY 8110 BL800

Why it fits. The core job in Harbor Desk is checking an identity: does the hull number match the record? That's where I/l/1, 0/O and B/8 get confused, and those are exactly the pairs this face was drawn to tell apart. Its quirks (the spurred l, the slashed zero, the open tails) carry the personality.

Who it serves. Marina staff outdoors in July sun on a dock tablet, and owners and operators who skew 50+. This choice puts the operator ahead of the portfolio.

System note. Its Mono sibling can replace IBM Plex Mono, so every ID in the product comes from one legibility-led family.

Watch. It's the quietest of the five at heading sizes. It needs 800 weight and tight tracking to feel designed rather than merely accessible.

04 · Chart room
Newsreader

Optical-size serif. Italic for water and vessels, the way nautical charts letter them

Production Type · 200–800 + italics · opsz 6–72 · Google Fonts, OFL
Ag300
Ag500
Ag700
Lake Effect600 italic

Why it fits. NOAA chart 14833, where your palette already comes from, letters water in italic serif and land in roman. Harbor Desk can follow the same rule: Buffalo River and Lake Erie on the 3D scene, and vessel names in italic because a boat's name is painted on its transom. Slip numbers and counts stay roman. That's a real convention, carried into software.

Who it serves. Managers and owners, people who navigate by paper charts and have read tide tables for decades. It's also the strongest first impression: every other ops dashboard is a grotesque.

Density. The serif only appears in names and headings, so it adds hierarchy without adding ink. It shows up three times per panel, and that's enough.

Watch. In a dark 3D UI a serif can drift toward editorial. Keep it out of controls, tables and buttons, and never set it below 16 px.

05 · Logbook
Recursive

One variable family with Linear, Mono and Casual axes, mapped to who wrote each record

Arrow Type, Stephen Nixon · weights 300–1000 · MONO, CASL, slnt, CRSV · Google Fonts, OFL
Ag300 linear
Ag800 linear
B-12500 mono
Hull no. ok500 casual

Why it fits. Harbor Desk already separates sources (DOC, SIM, ASSUME, FIELD). Recursive puts that into the type itself: system text is Linear, machine records (IDs, camera times) are Mono, and anything a person typed on the dock, like a staff evidence note, is Casual, so it looks like a logbook entry. You can tell who said it before you read it.

Who it serves. The dockmaster resolving the mismatch, who has to weigh a camera's claim against a coworker's note. It replaces Plex Mono too, which means one fewer family to load.

Hiring angle. Typography doing product work, not decoration. That's the kind of decision a principal designer gets asked about in a portfolio review.

Watch. Casual can turn cartoonish. Limit it to quoted staff notes. Recursive also reads a bit "developer tool", so let Work Sans carry the calm.

Recommendation: 04 Newsreader, with 01's labelling discipline

Newsreader gives the product a point of view that comes from the domain (chart lettering) rather than from a trend. It's the option most likely to stop a recruiter scrolling, and the hardest for a hiring manager to dismiss as a stock pick. It also suits the people who run marinas. Work Sans does the operating work, and the serif appears only where a name belongs.

Close second: 03 Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, if you want the case study to lead with operator empathy over visual flair. The wildcard is 05 Recursive, if the provenance story is the headline of your case study.
